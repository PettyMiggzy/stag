# Pad: Uniswap v4 launchpad on Robinhood Chain (quote token = $STAG)

Status: audited internally in repeated hostile rounds; NOT yet deployed; needs lawyer sign-off on the holder payout (peace mode) before mainnet.

## What it does
- `PadFactory.launch` makes a token, opens a $STAG / token v4 pool with `PadHook` attached and seeds the whole 1B supply as one-sided liquidity the factory owns and can never remove. The creator collects the pool's LP fee (0.30%). No launch minimum, no bonding curve. Minimum starting value is 1,000,000 $STAG.
- `PadHook` takes 3% of the $STAG leg of every swap. Buy tax: **war mode** -> the project's war chest; **peace mode** -> 1/3 creator, 2/3 holders (paid in $STAG through the token's dividend counter). Sell tax -> platform. The hook keeps everything as ERC-6909 claims at the PoolManager.
- A war chest can only buy the project's own token through its own pool and burn it. The creator burns whenever they like, at most one burn per pool per hour, each call limited to ~1.5% price impact.
- `PadBattle`: a creator challenges another project (their own pool locks while the offer is open), the opponent accepts, both pools lock, the clock runs. Higher $STAG volume since acceptance wins. Only what each pool earned DURING the fight is at stake: the loser's fight earnings move into the winner's chest, and the winner's fight earnings plus that loot are marked "forced" and get burned (creator first; strangers may burn it in steps). Older chest money stays with its pool. Ties move nothing.

## Rules worth knowing
- Where the $STAG side of a swap is specified, the swap must fill completely or it reverts (so tax and volume are always on what really traded). Swaps that fill nothing revert.
- Only the factory can add liquidity to a pool. Zero-liquidity pokes (fee collection) are allowed.
- Dividends are staged and credited at the first token interaction of a later block, so tokens borrowed for one transaction never earn.
- Hook owner powers: take the platform's sell-side share, hand ownership over in two steps, wire the factory and battle once.
- Names/symbols: letters, digits, space and `. _ - + ! ? : #` only. The site must still trim, collapse whitespace and flag duplicates/look-alikes.

## Known limits (accepted, documented)
- **Wash trading** can win battles; it costs the trader tax and fills chests.
- **One person can run both sides of a battle** and move the loser's fight earnings into the winner's chest. A creator already controls their own chest (they can choose never to burn it), so this adds no power over honest buyers; in audits it lost money in every measured case (burn cap 1.5%/hour, 3% sell tax). It cannot be prevented by any rule (second wallet).
- **A loser can try to force an exact tie** by topping up volume in the last second; a tie moves nothing.
- **A pool pumped to the top of its range** has nothing left to buy: burns revert, a forced chest then stays stuck and that creator cannot start the next fight until it can be burned.
- **`block.number` on this chain is the Ethereum (L1) block number** (Arbitrum-style): "a later block" for dividend crediting means a later L1 block, ~12 s, spanning many chain blocks. This is stricter than intended and flash-take protection still holds.
- Parked dividends (eligible supply under 1 token) go to whoever first holds a token across the next block boundary.
- Exact-in and exact-out trades differ by ~3% in effective tax; volume is the pool's actual $STAG leg.
- Third parties can create their own hook-less pool for the same token; it is outside this system.
- Starting price snaps to the pool's 60-tick grid in the creator's favour: the real start is at or slightly above the request (up to ~0.6%). In the token-is-currency0 ordering an adversarially chosen request can land up to 1 wei of STAG below it (integer square-root rounding); this cannot undercut the 1,000,000 STAG minimum.
- The PoolManager's owner can set a protocol fee (at most 0.1%) on any pool, pad pools included; it would slightly reduce creators' LP fees and has no effect on solvency.
- Aggregators that price from a pool's raw price alone will miss the 3% tax; quoters that simulate the hook price it correctly.
- Routers: standard v4 routers work; a router that syncs $STAG before the swap works (the holder flush steps aside).

## Mainnet deployment (see scripts/)
Rules of the road: never announce an address or send anyone to the pad until the FINAL readback (after the multisig accepts, with `EXPECT_OWNER_ACCEPTED=1`) passes. Note `factory.launch` is permissionless the moment the deployer lands, so third parties can launch before you do; that is harmless to the system but means "nothing has launched" is not guaranteed. The deployer key is a throwaway: fund it only for gas and discard it afterwards.

**Trust anchors you must obtain out of band** (the scripts cannot supply these): (a) the audited commit hash and the keccak digest of `audited-manifest.json` published with the audit report (`EXPECT_MANIFEST_DIGEST`; at the time of writing `0xca4321cc7077227dc7343a55106a19f0dfde057670594afbf14abbacaaf7ec5a`, but trust the published report, not this file); (b) the multisig address compared in the Safe app by the signers themselves (the scripts cannot prove the address is the one you intend: a look-alike contract is stopped only by the code-hash check below and by this human comparison), its owner list (`EXPECT_OWNERS`), threshold (`EXPECT_THRESHOLD`), the Safe implementation address (`EXPECT_SINGLETON`, from Safe's published deployments), the canonical SafeProxy runtime code hash (`EXPECT_PROXY_CODEHASH`, e.g. `cast keccak $(cast code <a proxy you created through the official Safe factory>)`, cross-checked against Safe's deployments repo), and the handler pair `EXPECT_FALLBACK` + `EXPECT_FALLBACK_CODEHASH` (only if the Safe has a fallback handler; it must be the official CompatibilityFallbackHandler: the scripts also refuse any handler that is one of this system's contracts, PM, STAG, the Safe itself or the deployer EOA, because the Safe forwards any unknown call to it as itself. Never copy the handler the script prints into the pin to make a row pass). Also pin the code, not just the addresses: `EXPECT_SINGLETON_CODEHASH` and `EXPECT_FACTORY_CODEHASH` (`cast keccak $(cast code <addr>)`, cross-checked against Safe's published deployments on a second machine). (c) the Safe's creation transaction hash (`EXPECT_SAFE_CREATION_TX`) and the official SafeProxyFactory address (`EXPECT_SAFE_FACTORY`, from Safe's published deployments): the scripts require that transaction to be sent TO that factory, that the factory logged exactly one `ProxyCreation` for the multisig with the pinned singleton (so only the factory's own CREATE2 of the canonical proxy code could have made it: no hostile constructor and no forged log), that exactly one `SafeSetup` from the multisig was logged before it with `initializer == 0` (so `setup()` made no delegatecall that could plant unlisted owners/modules or pre-approved hashes), and owners/threshold unchanged since. Create the Safe with `to = 0x0` in its setup (the plain `createProxyWithNonce` path; Safe app flows that use a `SafeToL2Setup` initializer will deliberately fail this check) and use a fresh Safe made for this purpose. (d) Compare the PoolManager and STAG addresses in `scripts/lib.js` with official sources and the STAG symbol/decimals on the explorer.

Run `node scripts/make-manifest.js` only after a reviewed source change.
1. **Pre-flight (read-only).** Chain id 4663 (compare the head block number and hash on two explorers); PoolManager `0x8366a39cc670b4001a1121b8f6a443a643e40951` and STAG `0x67Ef4460A1280fBD9d31D6312FEd2Ce1EFd66305` have code (hard-coded in the scripts); the multisig is a Safe whose threshold is at least 2, with no modules, no guard, and the verified singleton (deploy.js and readback.js check these), whose signers you have rehearsed with a no-op transaction (only you can prove the signers can sign).

EXPECT_FALLBACK_CODEHASH=.. EXPECT_MANIFEST_DIGEST=.. node scripts/readback.js`. All of those except EXPECT_FALLBACK and EXPECT_FALLBACK_CODEHASH (required together if the Safe has a handler) are mandatory on mainnet. It runs the whole readback on both RPCs (every ok/fail verdict must match), fetches the creation transaction and requires: sender and nonce equal to `salt.json`, plain contract creation, calldata that starts with the PINNED PadDeployer creation code (which embeds the hook, factory and battle creation code, constructors included) and ends with exactly (PM, STAG, MULTISIG, true, salt). It also requires the runtime hashes to match the manifest, the Safe checks, and the CREATE/CREATE2 derivations of all addresses. All rows PASS; WARNs read and understood. Independently re-verify the three addresses on a different machine (Blockscout read-contract or `cast call`).
6. **The multisig calls `hook.acceptOwner()`** promptly (until it does, the functionless PadDeployer is the owner and the platform share just accumulates). If the target was wrong, redeploy with a new salt.
7. **Readback after accept**: same command with `EXPECT_OWNER_ACCEPTED=1`. Then canary launches (war and peace, both currency orderings), small buys and sells in all four variants, a burn, `collectFees`, a battle, solvency check after each step; publish addresses; verify source on the Blockscout explorer (`https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com`).
Solvency monitor: `PoolManager.balanceOf(hook, uint160(STAG)) >= platformOwed + sum(chest + creatorOwed + holderOwed)`.

## Tests
`RH_BLOCK=<near head> npx hardhat test` (mainnet fork). `npx hardhat --config hardhat.nolimit.config.js test test/deploy.dryrun.test.js` for the deployment dry run.

RPC independence cannot be enforced by a script (the script only checks that the host names differ): choose two genuinely separate providers, ideally one your own node. The readback loops over every launch on both endpoints, so run it before announcing the pad.

Residual co-signer risk (no script can remove it): any owner of the Safe can `approveHash` for future nonces, owners can be contracts that sign without a key, and the signers collectively control the Safe. Use only signers you trust, hardware keys, and a threshold of at least 2. `deploy.js` checks the Safe on one RPC only (the default public one unless `RH_RPC` is set); the dual-RPC readback before `acceptOwner` is the real gate. Use `https` RPC URLs.

**Creating the Safe** (plain path, `to = 0x0`): call the official SafeProxyFactory's `createProxyWithNonce(singleton, setupData, saltNonce)` directly (for example with `cast send`), where `setupData = setup(owners, threshold, address(0), 0x, fallbackHandler, address(0), 0, address(0))` and `fallbackHandler` is `0` or the official CompatibilityFallbackHandler. Safes made through the Safe app use a `SafeToL2Setup` initializer and enable a module by default, so they fail the strict checks on purpose. `EXPECT_PROXY_CODEHASH` is the keccak of the runtime code of a proxy made by that same factory (any proxy it created has the same code). Check whether the Safe app/transaction service supports chain 4663 before you rely on it; if not, collect signatures with `approveHash` or offline EIP-712 signatures through a script, and rehearse that path.

**Rehearsal and the accept transaction**: the no-op rehearsal must be signed by EVERY owner (not only a threshold subset), so a counterfactual or key-less owner slot is exposed. When signing `acceptOwner`, each signer verifies on their device: `to` = the hook address, `data` = `0x238ac933` (`acceptOwner()`), `operation` = Call (never DelegateCall), `value` = 0. Keep `salt.json` and the creation tx hash: steps 5 and 7 need them. The audited commit and manifest digest should be recorded in a place the repo does not control (the audit report you hold), not only in this file.

**If launches spam the factory** before your readback: the readback scan is bounded (`SCAN_MAX`, default 300) and batched; above it, the solvency scan is skipped with a WARN and you must verify solvency with your own monitor before announcing. Ownership, code and wiring checks do not depend on the number of launches. If the deployer's target turns out wrong after launches exist, a redeploy leaves the old system live with its `pendingOwner` set: nobody can accept it except that target, so tell nobody to use it.
