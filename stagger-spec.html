Stagger — v1 Build Spec

The CRM for junk removal & moving crews, with $STAG on the rails. This spec defines the smallest version that runs a real job end-to-end — so we can ship it, dogfood it on a real North Houston operation, and film it. Everything not required for that loop is explicitly deferred.

v1 · MVP Niche: junk removal & moving Customer #1: our own company

01TL;DR

Build the smallest thing that takes a customer from lead → estimate → scheduled job → invoice → paid, with a clean mobile-first UI a crew can run from a truck. Payments work normally (card) and can settle in $STAG/ETH. $STAG lives on the owner side (subscription + perks), so customers never need a wallet. We are customer #1; the content engine is the growth channel.

The one job of v1

Run a real paid job through Stagger, on video, without falling back to paper or another app. If v1 can't do that, nothing else matters yet.

02Guiding principles

  • Vertical, not horizontal. Every label, default, and flow assumes junk removal / moving — not "field service in general."
  • Dogfood first. Our own company is the only user that matters for v1. Build what that operation actually needs each day.
  • $STAG on the owner side. The business pays for Stagger and earns perks in $STAG; end customers pay however they want. Zero wallet friction for the couch-haul customer.
  • Mobile-first. The crew lives on a phone in a driveway. Desktop is for the back office.
  • Low backend, boring stack. Reuse the existing Neon Postgres. No microservices, no premature scale.
  • Ship the loop, defer the polish. Route optimization, reviews automation, payroll — all v2.

05The core loop

Lead / client→Estimate→Customer approves→Scheduled job→Work done→Invoice→Paid
  1. Add a client (name, phone, address) — or it's created inline while making an estimate.
  2. Build an estimate — line items (e.g., "single item," "half load," "full load," "labor / hr"), a total, optional photos. One tap to send by SMS/email.
  3. Customer opens the link (no login) → sees a clean branded estimate → taps Approve. Owner gets notified.
  4. Schedule it — drop the approved job on a day/time, assign a crew.
  5. Do the job — crew marks On the way → Started → Done, snaps before/after photos.
  6. Invoice — one tap turns the estimate into an invoice (adjust if the load grew), sends a pay link.
  7. Get paid — customer pays by card or $STAG/ETH; job auto-marks Paid. Done.

07Screens

Owner / crew app (auth'd, mobile-first)

  • Today — today's jobs, statuses, quick actions.
  • Clients — list + search, client detail with job history.
  • New estimate — pick/create client → add line items → send.
  • Schedule — simple day/week calendar, drag a job onto a slot, assign crew.
  • Job detail — status stepper, photos, estimate/invoice, "Send invoice," "Collect payment."
  • Settings — business name, logo, brand color, payout wallet, default line-item presets, $STAG perks.

Customer (public, no login)

  • Estimate view /e/<token> — branded estimate + Approve / Decline.
  • Pay view /pay/<token> — invoice + Pay by card or Pay with crypto ($STAG/ETH).
  • Receipt — confirmation + on-chain link if paid in crypto.

08Payments & $STAG

Two rails, one invoice

  • Card / ACH — via a processor (Stripe or similar). This is the default so any customer can pay. (Requires a merchant account; see decisions.)
  • Crypto ($STAG / ETH on Robinhood Chain) — the pay page shows a wallet-connect + exact amount; on confirmation we record tx_hash and auto-mark paid. Instant settlement, no processor fee.

Where $STAG actually creates demand

$STAG is on the owner side
  • Subscription in $STAG — businesses pay their Stagger plan in $STAG (or get a discount for doing so). Recurring buy pressure from real revenue.
  • Perk tiers — hold/stake $STAG → lower platform fee, higher limits, premium features (stag_perks_tier).
  • Optional crypto settlement — owners who want it can take customer payments straight to their wallet, or auto-convert card revenue toward $STAG.

The end customer is never forced into crypto — that's what keeps adoption frictionless while still driving real, recurring $STAG volume from businesses.

10Build phases

Phase 1 · Loop skeleton

Clients → estimate → approve → invoice → mark paid (manual)

No real payments yet — just the data model, the auth'd app, and the two public link pages. Prove the flow end-to-end. Milestone: run a fake job start to finish.

Phase 2 · Get paid for real

Card + crypto on the pay page

Wire Stripe and WalletConnect/$STAG payment, auto-mark paid, receipt with on-chain link. Milestone: take a real $ payment on a real job (film it).

Phase 3 · Crew + schedule

Calendar, crew accounts, job statuses, photos

Make it a daily driver for the crew, not just the owner. Milestone: the crew runs a full day out of Stagger.

Phase 4 · $STAG owner layer

Subscription in $STAG + perk tiers

Turn on the business-side $STAG economics. Milestone: first business pays its plan in $STAG.

11Out of scope (deliberately)

Named so nobody sneaks them into v1: route optimization, automated review/referral engine, public online booking, recurring jobs, payroll/timesheets, dump-fee & inventory tracking, multi-business/franchise, AI receptionist, marketing automation, QuickBooks sync. All real, all v2+.

12Open decisions (need answers to start)

  1. Card processor & entity. Taking customer money means a merchant account and likely an LLC + money-handling terms. Who's the merchant of record — your junk-removal LLC, or a new Stagger entity? This gates Phase 2.
  2. Crypto settlement default. Do crypto payments go straight to the owner's wallet, or through a Stagger contract that takes a small fee in $STAG? (The latter is where platform revenue + burn can live.)
  3. Domain. stagger.app / its own domain, or stagger.stagwifhood.fun for v1?
  4. Line-item presets. Give me your real junk-removal pricing tiers (min charge, 1/4 / 1/2 / full load, labor rate, moving hourly) so the estimate builder ships with your defaults.
  5. Who builds it. Is the dev taking this, am I speccing for them, or do you want me to start Phase 1 scaffolding?
